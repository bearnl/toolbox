# Non-depth "privacy-preserving" modalities that machine-identify people — COMPLETE (sub-agent of the thesis audit; verbatim, 2026-09-06)

Verification legend: [V] = citation/numbers confirmed on the DOI/arXiv/venue page or via Crossref/Europe PMC/S2; "(unverified)" = memory/snippet only. Web-search budget ran out mid-task.

## 0. Bottom line
1. **Closest pre-emption — a preprint posted five days ago: Todt, Morsbach, Dissert, Strufe, "MultiGait: A Multi-Sensor Multi-Perspective Multi-Session Biometric Inference Benchmark and its Dataset", arXiv:2609.01036 (1 Sep 2026) [V].** 199 people, 8 sensors (RGB, depth, NIR, LWIR thermal, LiDAR, mmWave radar, WiFi CSI, WiFi BFI), 4 viewpoints, 3 sessions (Nov 2024 / Feb 2025 / Mar 2025; 135 once, 36 twice, 28 three times), session-disjoint evaluation (session 2 held out). As read from the arXiv HTML: within-session rank-1 >98 % for video/depth/NIR/LWIR/LiDAR; radar ~81 % on a 10-person subset, 20–64 % full set; CSI 41–91 %; BFI 35–82 %. Cross-session: 60–80 % for video/depth/NIR/LWIR/LiDAR; radar/CSI/BFI near chance (<20 %); chance ≈ 0.5 %. Framing: sensors "assumed to be privacy-friendly do still entail considerable identity inference risks"; unsubstantiated claims "foster a false sense of privacy". Overlap: PRE-EMPTS (body-scale, depth+thermal+LiDAR+RF, cross-session, privacy framing). It does NOT (as far as readable) do 2.5-s clips, precision-reduction/person-masking ablations, or a per-person identifiability distribution — those remain the manuscript's differentiators. Re-check its tables directly.
2. VR telemetry (Nair USENIX Sec'23; Miller Sci Rep'20; Rack'23; Liebers CHI'21) already argues "human-illegible motion streams are biometrics" at scale — active VR, not passive ambient sensors.
3. Skeletons: Moon et al. AAAI 2023 (80 % re-ID; privacy framing); Carr et al. 2024 ("may appear to be anonymous").
4. Event cameras: Ahmad ICCV'23 / Bendig WACV'25 / Müller CVPR-W'26 already argue the "privacy-preserving sensor" assumption is wrong.
5. Closest analogues to the manuscript's two ablations: (a) Nair et al. 2024 — noise, frame-rate, PRECISION and dimensionality reductions on VR motion; attacks "still achieve near-perfect accuracy"; (b) Wu et al. ECCV 2018 — on SBU Kinect RGB, degradations still "reveal significant amounts of identity information"; only cropping the whole body removes identity (and kills action recognition).
6. Nobody frames it as "illegible to humans ≠ anonymous to machines"; nearest: MultiGait "false sense of privacy", Kröger 2019 "unexpected inferences", BDQ ECCV 2022 (machine attacker vs human-observer study).

## 1. Skeleton / pose / motion
1.1 Nair et al., "Unique Identification of 50,000+ VR Users from Head & Hand Motion Data," USENIX Security 2023, arXiv:2302.08927 [V] — 55,541 users; 94.33 % from 100 s, 73.20 % from 10 s; multi-session. PRE-EMPTS (general claim).
1.2 Miller et al., Sci. Rep. 10:17404 (2020), DOI 10.1038/s41598-020-74486-y [V] — 511 participants; RF 95.0 %; same-day. PRE-EMPTS (framing) / PARTIAL.
1.3 Moon et al., "Anonymization for Skeleton Action Recognition," AAAI 2023, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v37i12.26754 [V] — re-ID 80 %, gender 87 %. PRE-EMPTS for skeletons.
1.4 Carr, Xu, Lu, "Adversary-Guided Motion Retargeting for Skeleton Anonymization," arXiv:2405.05428 [V]. PARTIAL.
1.5 Castro, Hanisch, Fallahi, Strufe, "FacialMotionID," arXiv:2507.11138 [V] — 116 people, 3 sessions; up to 98 %. PRE-EMPTS framing; face-scale.
1.6 **Nair et al., "Effect of Data Degradation on Motion Re-Identification," SePAR@WoWMoM 2024, arXiv:2407.18378 [V]** — precision reduction etc. does not stop identification. Closest analogue to "reducing depth precision does not help" — cite.
1.7 Zhao, "Towards Frame-Level Person Identification Using Kinect Skeleton Data with Deep Learning," IEEE SSCI 2021, DOI 10.1109/SSCI50451.2021.9659996 [V] — 164 subjects; >90 % from a SINGLE skeleton frame (static proportions). Supports "body shape is what leaks".
1.8 Rack et al., "Who is Alyx?" Frontiers VR 2023, DOI 10.3389/frvir.2023.1272234 [V] — 71 users, two sessions, 95 % cross-session. Frizzo et al., arXiv:2604.16341 (2026) benchmark.
1.9 Liebers et al., CHI 2021, DOI 10.1145/3411764.3445528 [V] — 16 participants; up to 90 % across days; body normalisation changes accuracy by up to 38 %.
1.10 Miller et al., arXiv:2303.01430 (2023); Miller, Nair et al., SePAR 2024, arXiv:2407.18380 [V] — 232 users, 8 weekly sessions; longer train–test delay lowers identifiability; control delay in privacy evaluations.
