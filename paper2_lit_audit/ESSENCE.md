## Thesis

Depth cameras are installed in lavatories, care bedrooms, ICUs and hospital corridors on a stated premise: a depth frame is anonymous because nobody can recognise anyone in it — "sensing only depth maps" (Ballester 2024, toileting assistance), "prevent identification of the person" (Luo 2018, assisted living), "not actual cameras" (Singh 2020, hospital), "inherently preserves privacy", used to waive ethics review (Delécluse et al., FG 2025). No regulator has ever said so; the premise lives in engineering practice. That "obfuscated for humans is not anonymous to machines" is an old principle — faces (Newton 2005; McPherson 2016), bodies in RGB (Oh 2016), and now sensors at scale (MultiGait 2026) — and the paper does not claim it. It claims the **depth-specific mechanism**: why the near-ceiling numbers in the closed-set depth literature, our own 2023 0.99 included, are a property of the evaluation split; what in the frame carries the identity that survives a leak-free protocol; what a deployment actually obtains from two and a half seconds of depth with the sensor's own segmentation; and that two would-be privacy controls — reducing depth precision, masking the person out — are not controls. What leaks is the outline.

## Position against MultiGait (Todt, Morsbach, Dissert, Strufe; arXiv:2609.01036, 1 Sep 2026)

Cite it first and prominently as the scale result: 199 people, eight sensors, three sessions; silhouette gait recognisers on Azure Kinect depth reach 100 % within a session and 65–77 % across a one-to-three-month gap (GaitBase; probes ≤ 64 people against a 199-identity gallery). Three facts from the primary text make the complement position exact. (i) It never evaluates depth values: its "depth" is a size-normalised binary silhouette from empty-room subtraction, and its authors note that "the actual sensing technology has little impact" once silhouettes exist — direct corroboration of our thesis. (ii) Its single-session split is per whole walk (≈ our R3): no frame-random rung, no guard sweep, no trivial-cue floor, no attribution, no bit-depth axis, no observation budget, no per-person distribution. (iii) It names cross-session generalisation an open gap. The same group's "A False Sense of Privacy" (PoPETs 2024) is the methodological precedent for C1. Never write "on 199 people" for the cross-session figure, never rank our 43–47 % against their 65–77 % (chance 3.6 % vs 0.5 %; 2.5 s vs whole walks; one training recording vs 120–240 samples), and never cite their depth > video result for C4 (both streams are silhouettes there).

## The four contributions, in their audit-narrowed form

**C1 — Protocol.** A leak-free evaluation ladder for closed-set person identification from video: R0 frame-random → R1 block hold-out with a *temporal guard-band sweep at matched training size* → R3 cross-recording → R4 cross-session, read against a *metadata-only floor* (13 bounding-box scalars + random forest) and a per-rung permutation null. One model, one dataset, only the split changes: depth 98.4 → 62.3 → 34.8 → 6.7 %; RGB 99.99 → 91.9 → 79.5 → 5.6 %; the floor 89.4 → 17.7 → 8.5 → 5.4 %. Replicated on three corpora and three sensor technologies (BIWI Kinect v1; in-house Azure Kinect; TVRID RealSense D455: depth 62.6 → 13.5 → 8.4 → 6.8 % on 86/88 identities): frame-random overstates block-held-out accuracy 5–9× in every one. *Defensible novelty:* no prior guard-band sweep at matched n for person identification in any modality; no prior bounding-box-only identity floor; the first depth study to report every rung on the same data; the first quantified inflation for vision-based *human* identification (prior quantifications: sea turtles, touch, ECG, PPG, EEG, MRI slices, HAR). *Not claimed:* that same-session inflation is a discovery (Hammerla & Plötz 2015; Saeb 2017; Li 2021; Georgiev 2022; SeaTurtleID 2024; MultiGait); that the block split or guard band is new (h-block CV 1994; hv-block 2000; blocked CV 2012); that R3→R4 measures "time" (Matovski 2012: time alone is nearly neutral — it is clothing, room and framing); the first graded split hierarchy in vision (Woo, May 2026).

**C2 — Attribution.** What the accuracy is *of*, by single controlled edits at every rung: person-only; the pixel-exact complement (person inpainted out from that recording's own plate — no silhouette-shaped hole); hole kept; re-centred; size+position removed; binary silhouette; and a Z-precision axis 16 → 1 bit at fixed metric range with the shipped silhouette as the 1-bit limit. Within session the room beats the person (R3: plate 38.6 % vs full 34.8 % vs person 8.8 %); across the session change the background-carrying inputs fall to the null and the framing-normalised person is what remains (R4: person 9.2, centred 11.3, size+position removed 13.6, normalised silhouette 13.0 — an ordering across many cells, not individually significant contrasts, and the text says so). 2 bits ≈ 16 bits at R4; 1 bit 9.5 %. The RGB cross-session loss is person-borne. *Defensible novelty:* first depth person-ID study with the exact-complement pair at fixed model and protocol, ladder-resolved; the Z-precision axis, "to our knowledge"; two negative privacy-control results in depth (hole ≠ control; precision reduction ≠ control — no depth de-identification or identity-leakage evaluation exists anywhere). *Not claimed:* novelty of background/person ablations or of inpainting as a control (Xiao 2021; Tian 2018; HAT 2022); depth clothing-robustness (Wu 2017; Karianakis 2018; Castro 2020); any general "the CNN reads outline, not 3-D shape" — contradicted at fixed encoder by LidarGait 2023, Hofmann 2014 and Haque 2016 on BIWI itself; scoped to single-frame Kinect v1 and this architecture.

**C3 — Measurement validity and the operating point.** Metric anthropometry in millimetres from unprojected depth and the sensor mask is the strongest single-frame cross-session cue *among the models we train* (19.0 % [14.0, 24.4] vs the CNN's 6.7 %). 74 % of walking frames clip the body and `stature` drifts 89.5 mm per metre of range against a 105 mm between-subject SD, so a validity gate is part of the measurement (28.1 % on complete-body frames) — gates have been applied since Barbosa 2012 and never ablated; we quantify one. With the gate and 2.5 s of observation (W = 25, 103 decisions): CNN 28.2 % [14.8, 43.0], metric 43.3 % [30.3, 56.1], fusion 47.0 % [32.9, 61.4]; fusion − metric +3.7 pp [−13.8, +20.4] is **not resolvable** at n = 28, fusion − CNN +18.8 [+9.0, +29.4] is. Top-3 81 %. Per-subject identifiability 0–100 % (3 of 28 never, 3 always, median 40 %) — the biometric menagerie (Doddington 1998; Yager & Dunstone 2010) in depth. Cohort curve K = 4 → 28: 83.9 → 43.3 %, with a 58-pp spread between draws at K = 4 — *which* people are enrolled matters more than how many (an empirical observation; the curve's shape follows Grother & Phillips 2004 / Friedman 2022; Andersson 2015 measured it within-session). **The reviewer's comparison the paper must make itself:** depth-only cross-session identification on these same 28 BIWI people was published in 2014–2018 at 21–30 % single-shot and 43–50 % multi-shot (Munaro; Haque 3D/4D-RAM; Wu; Karianakis). Our single-frame numbers sit below that band and our 2.5-s operating point inside it. We do not improve on those numbers and say so; the paper's question is which measurement conditions produce them, and the mechanistic explanation (full frame carrying the room; matched n_train; one training recording; a 2.5-s budget instead of whole sequences) is exactly the C2/C3 story. Chance and decision-space size are stated on every table (28-way = 3.57 %; 50-way = 2.0 %; human performance on BIWI depth 6.7 %, Haque 2016).

**C4 — Modality.** RGB's advantage over depth is clothing appearance and protocol-dependent: +19.5 / +38.7 / +33.4 / +1.3 pp mean paired difference at R0 / R1 / R3 / R4; on TVRID, where clothes do not change between passages, it persists (+25 pp). RGB with its own DeepLabV3 segmenter equals RGB with the sensor mask (median IoU 0.74; −1.7 to +4.0 pp, within seed variance), so the masked-RGB rows describe an RGB-only system. Presented as a replication — Hafner 2022 (+44.5 pp within-instance on BIWI), Karianakis 2018 on TUM-GAID (depth > RGB across clothes), PRCC/LTCC (RGB collapse under clothing change) — extended to three sensor technologies with the same pipeline and the segmenter control, for which no prior use was found.

**Negative results — a section, not an appendix.** Score-level, distance-term and feature-level fusion all fail to realise a measured oracle complementarity of +7.7–9.0 pp; at K = 7 retrained fusion (54.0 %) sits below metric alone (70.3 %). Gait is untestable on BIWI, with the measured reason. The per-identity duplication → R0→R1-drop correlation is refuted (ρ = +0.64 / +0.78, the wrong sign, because ratio and drop are both room properties). The 2023 efficiency claims are arithmetically false (the head was the size, not the channel count). Two hypotheses pre-registered and refuted (score fusion; metric scale).

## What will not be claimed
