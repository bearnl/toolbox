## Thesis

Depth cameras are installed in lavatories, care bedrooms, ICUs and hospital corridors on a stated premise: a depth frame is anonymous because nobody can recognise anyone in it — "sensing only depth maps" (Ballester 2024, toileting assistance), "prevent identification of the person" (Luo 2018, assisted living), "not actual cameras" (Singh 2020, hospital), "inherently preserves privacy", used to waive ethics review (Delécluse et al., FG 2025). No regulator text examined addresses depth or 3-D body sensing as a modality, and none declares any sensor non-identifying; the premise lives in engineering practice. That "obfuscated for humans is not anonymous to machines" is an old principle — faces (Newton 2005; McPherson 2016), bodies in RGB (Oh 2016), sensors at scale (MultiGait 2026) — and the paper does not claim it. It claims the **depth-specific mechanism**: why the near-ceiling numbers in the closed-set depth literature, our own 2023 0.99 included, are a property of the evaluation split; what in the frame carries the identity that survives a leak-free protocol; what a deployment actually obtains from two and a half seconds of depth with the sensor's own segmentation; and that two controls practitioners reach for — reducing depth-value precision, masking the person out — are not privacy controls. What leaks is the outline.

## Position against MultiGait (Todt, Morsbach, Dissert, Strufe; arXiv:2609.01036, 1 Sep 2026)

Cite it first and prominently as the scale result: 199 people, eight sensors, three sessions; silhouette gait recognisers on Azure Kinect depth reach 100 % within a session and 65–77 % across a one-to-three-month gap (GaitBase; probes ≤ 64 people against a 199-identity gallery). Three facts from the primary text make the complement position exact. (i) It never evaluates depth values: its "depth" is a size-normalised binary silhouette from empty-room subtraction, and its authors note that "the actual sensing technology has little impact" once silhouettes exist — direct corroboration of our thesis. (ii) Its single-session split is per whole walk (≈ our R3, not frame-random): no frame-random rung, no guard sweep, no trivial-cue floor, no attribution, no bit-depth axis, no observation budget, no per-person distribution. (iii) It names cross-session generalisation an open gap. The same group's "A False Sense of Privacy" (PoPETs 2024) is the methodological precedent for C1. Never write "on 199 people" for the cross-session figure; never rank our 43–47 % against their 65–77 % (chance 3.6 % vs 0.5 %; 2.5 s vs whole walks; one training recording vs 120–240 samples); never cite their depth > video result for C4 (both streams are silhouettes there). **And MultiGait is not the first depth within/across pair: TUM-GAID (Hofmann et al., JVCIR 2014, Table 6) reported depth-GEI 96.8 → 28 %, DGHEI 99.0 → 50 % same-session → three months later on one corpus in 2014.** The paper cites both as the endpoints and positions itself as the resolution of what lies between them.

## The four contributions, in the wording the refutation passes allow

**C1 — Protocol.** A leak-free evaluation ladder for closed-set person identification from video: R0 frame-random → R1 block hold-out with a *within-recording temporal guard-band sweep at matched training size* → R3 cross-recording → R4 cross-session, read against a *detector-metadata-only floor* (13 bounding-box scalars, no pixel or depth content) and a per-rung permutation null. One model, one dataset, only the split changes: depth 98.4 → 62.3 → 34.8 → 6.7 %; RGB 99.99 → 91.9 → 79.5 → 5.6 %; the floor 89.4 → 17.7 → 8.5 → 5.4 %. Replicated on three corpora spanning structured-light, time-of-flight and active-stereo sensors (TVRID: depth 62.6 → 13.5 → 8.4 → 6.8 % on 86/88 identities): frame-random overstates block-held-out accuracy 5–9× in every one. *Written as:* same-session vs cross-session inflation has been quantified for vision-based human identification since HumanID 2005 and TUM-GAID 2014, at scale by MultiGait 2026, and swept train–test delays exist for VR telemetry (Miller 2024) and ECG (Scagnetto 2026, constant gallery, 1–5 years); graded ladders exist for EEG, ECG, intrinsic decomposition and wildlife re-ID. What has not been done is to resolve the within-session number itself: to our knowledge the first *within-recording* guard-band sweep at matched n_train for person identification, the first detector-metadata-only identity floor, a cross-recording rung separated from the cross-session rung on one pipeline, and the first depth study to report the full ladder on the same data. *Not claimed:* that the leak is a discovery (Hammerla & Plötz 2015; Saeb 2017; Li 2021; Georgiev 2022 — 470 users; SeaTurtleID 2024); that the block split or guard band is new (h-block CV 1994; hv-block 2000; blocked CV 2012); that "the depth field never saw its inflated number" (Patruno 2019 published a frame-level 97.84 % on BIWI; TUM-GAID published both endpoints) — say instead that the R0 number was never placed beside the cross-day number on one pipeline; that R3 → R4 measures "time" (Matovski 2012); the first graded hierarchy in vision (Woo 2026). The anthropometric-scalar lineage of top-view depth re-ID (Liciotti 2017; Paolanti 2018) is cited as the floor's ancestor.

**C2 — Attribution.** What the accuracy is *of*, by single controlled edits at every rung: person-only; the pixel-exact complement (person inpainted out from that recording's own plate — no silhouette-shaped hole); hole kept; re-centred; size+position removed; binary silhouette; and a *depth-value* precision axis 16 → 1 bit at fixed metric range with the shipped silhouette as the 1-bit limit. Within session the room beats the person (R3: plate 38.6 % vs full 34.8 % vs person 8.8 %); across the session change the background-carrying inputs fall to the null and the framing-normalised person is what remains (R4: person 9.2, centred 11.3, size+position removed 13.6, normalised silhouette 13.0 — an ordering across many cells, not individually significant contrasts, and the text says so). 2 bits ≈ 16 bits at R4; 1 bit 9.5 %. *Written as:* background-only and person-only controls are standard in RGB recognition (Xiao 2021; He 2016; HAT 2022), were used once for RGB re-ID with a mean-filled person (Tian 2018) and recently with generative inpainting as a leakage ratio for wildlife re-ID (Rueda-Toicen 2026); RealGait (Zhang 2023) swept the *intensity* precision of the person region from the binary silhouette to 8-bit grey for RGB gait; person-cropped RGB failing under a clothing change is established on our own corpora (Wu 2017; Karianakis 2018). To our knowledge this is the first depth person-identification study to report the complement, the first in any modality to fill the person from the recording's own plate so that the two conditions are pixel-exact complements, the first to resolve the background's share at every rung (it changes sign across the session change), the first to vary the precision of the depth value itself, and the first to show the *background-only* condition does not carry RGB's cross-session loss — so the loss is person-borne. Hole ≠ control and precision reduction ≠ control are known constructs in RGB and motion data (Xiao 2021; Nair 2024; Hanisch 2023); we show them for depth at fixed model and protocol. Background-only *exceeding* the full frame (R3) has no found precedent in any modality; background *preference* does (Xiao 2021; Balieva 2026). *Not claimed:* any general "the CNN reads outline, not 3-D shape" — contradicted at fixed encoder by LidarGait 2023, Hofmann 2014, Haque 2016; scoped to single-frame Kinect v1 and this architecture. *Must add to Methods:* how the complement is constructed at the mask boundary (Kinect v1's invalid-pixel shadow around the person), with a difference image.

**C3 — Measurement validity and the operating point.** Metric anthropometry in millimetres from unprojected frontal depth through the sensor mask, without a skeleton tracker, is the strongest single-frame cross-session cue *among the models we train* (19.0 % [14.0, 24.4] vs the CNN's 6.7 %). 74 % of walking frames clip the body and `stature` drifts 89.5 mm per metre of range against a 105 mm between-subject SD, so a validity gate is part of the measurement (28.1 % on complete-body frames). With the gate and 2.5 s of observation (W = 25, 103 decisions): CNN 28.2 % [14.8, 43.0], metric 43.3 % [30.3, 56.1], fusion 47.0 % [32.9, 61.4]; fusion − metric +3.7 pp [−13.8, +20.4] is **not resolvable** at n = 28, fusion − CNN +18.8 [+9.0, +29.4] is. Top-3 81 %. Per-subject identifiability 0–100 % (median 40 %). Cohort curve K = 4 → 28: 83.9 → 43.3 %, draw spread 58 pp at K = 4. *Written as:* anthropometric soft biometrics from consumer depth are established (Barbosa 2012 — cross-day, height most informative; Munaro 2014; Andersson & Araujo 2015; skeleton-free top-view distances in Paolanti 2018; height beating colour in John 2013) — the mechanism is not our discovery; frame gates have been applied since Barbosa 2012 and Karianakis 2018 showed learned frame weights add 4.3 pp on BIWI; accuracy-vs-observation-length is a standard gait ablation (GaitSet; LidarGait; Rao 2022 on BIWI skeletons); gallery-size curves with random re-draws exist within session (Andersson 2015; Delécluse 2025); per-subject heterogeneity is the biometric menagerie (Doddington 1998; Yager & Dunstone 2010). What we add: the first measurement of how often the sensor's field of view clips the walking body and what that does to height features; an explicit mechanism-derived full-body gate ablated across sessions; depth-image accuracy against a wall-clock budget; and the first cross-session depth cohort curve with draw spread and per-subject distribution — the spread between random cohorts of one size exceeds the effect of size itself, which privacy assessments based on cohort size alone would miss. **The comparison the paper must make itself:** depth-only cross-session identification on these same 28 BIWI people was published in 2014–2018 at 21–30 % single-shot and 43–50 % multi-shot (Munaro; Haque 3D/4D-RAM; Wu; Karianakis). Our single-frame numbers sit below that band and our 2.5-s operating point inside it; we do not improve on them and say so. Wave 22 (the field's Training(50) → Walking / Still protocol, single- and multi-shot, same probe frames as R4) puts the two side by side on our own pipeline, and the mechanistic explanation — full frame carrying the room, matched n_train, one training recording, a 2.5-s budget — is the C2/C3 story. Chance, decision-space size and human performance (6.7 %, Haque 2016, four observers) are stated on every table.

**C4 — Modality.** RGB's advantage over depth is clothing appearance and protocol-dependent: +19.5 / +38.7 / +33.4 / +1.3 pp mean paired difference at R0 / R1 / R3 / R4; on TVRID, where clothes do not change between passages, it persists (+25 pp). RGB with its own DeepLabV3 segmenter equals RGB with the sensor mask (median IoU 0.74; −1.7 to +4.0 pp, within seed variance), so the within-session advantage is not a segmentation artefact and the masked-RGB rows describe an RGB-only system. *Written as:* a replication — Hafner 2022 (+44.5 pp within-instance on BIWI), Karianakis 2018 and Castro 2020 on TUM-GAID (depth outlasts RGB across a clothing change), PRCC/LTCC (RGB collapse), Delécluse 2025 (one pipeline across three depth technologies, within session) — extended to RGB-only vs depth-only across sessions on three sensor technologies with, to our knowledge, the first mask-source-equalised control.

**Negative results — a section, not an appendix.** Score-level, distance-term and feature-level fusion all fail to realise a measured oracle complementarity of +7.7–9.0 pp; at K = 7 retrained fusion (54.0 %) sits below metric alone (70.3 %) — reported as a discrepancy with the positive depth+RGB fusion results on TUM-GAID (Hofmann 2014; Castro 2020) and Wu 2017's shape+skeleton fusion. Gait could not be segmented under our protocol — single 2.5-s probes in which 74 % of frames clip the body — *not* "gait is untestable on BIWI": Haque 2016 report GEI 21.4 % and GEV 25.7 % multi-shot on whole sequences. The per-identity duplication → R0→R1-drop correlation is refuted (ρ = +0.64 / +0.78, the wrong sign, because ratio and drop are both room properties). The 2023 efficiency claims are arithmetically false. Two hypotheses pre-registered and refuted (score fusion; metric scale).

## What will not be claimed

SOTA; open-set re-ID; skeletons (paper 1); "depth is under-explored"; model size or speed; "depth is privacy-preserving" as a premise; "depth identifies people across sessions" as a discovery (Wu, Karianakis, Haque, TUM-GAID, MultiGait); "illegible ≠ anonymous" as a new principle; "as identifying as RGB" (Mucha 2022 and our own C4 say otherwise); "fusion adds 6 pp"; "false sense of privacy" as a title phrase (Hanisch 2024 owns it in the target venue); "first to catalogue contradictory depth-privacy claims" (Mucha & Kampel 2022 §2 did — we extend to sixteen verified deployments and two same-institution contradictions); "metric anthropometry identifies people" as a mechanism discovery (Barbosa 2012); anything about actual bathroom recordings; any categorical legal status of body shape; "no regulator mentions thermal/3-D" (CNIL 2020 has thermal-camera guidance; WP193 mentions 3-D anti-spoofing) — only "none addresses depth/3-D body sensing as a modality".
