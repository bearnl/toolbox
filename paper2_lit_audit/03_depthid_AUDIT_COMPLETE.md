## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. **Depth-only cross-session identification on BIWI is a 2014–2018 result with published numbers HIGHER than the manuscript's.** Same probe set (the 28 re-recorded people, new day, new clothes), gallery/training = session-1 "Training": single-shot rank-1 21.1 % (Munaro skeleton), 22.4 % (Munaro point cloud), 24.47 % (Wu ED+SKL, Walking), 25.4 % (Karianakis CNN), 30.1 % (Haque 3D RAM); multi-shot 42.9 % (Munaro PCM+skeleton), 45.3 % (Haque 4D RAM), 50.0 % (Karianakis RTA). The manuscript's R4 depth CNN (7 %) and single-frame anthropometry (19 %) sit BELOW all of these; its gated + 2.5-s operating point (43–47 %) sits INSIDE the prior multi-shot band. A reviewer will put these side by side. The manuscript must (a) report its decision-space size and chance level (28-way = 3.6 %; 50-way softmax = 2.0 %), (b) add the standard "full Training → Walking" rung with its best person-only / silhouette input so numbers are commensurable, and (c) explain the gap mechanistically (full-frame input carrying background, matched n_train, 2.5-s clips, no body-index crop) — which is exactly the C2 story, so this is an opportunity, not only a threat.
2. **Hand-crafted metric anthropometry beating "every CNN" must be scoped to the manuscript's own CNNs.** Karianakis 2018 and Haque 2016 state the opposite in print ("end-to-end features perform significantly better than ... hand-crafted biometrics"), and on BIWI Wu's hand-crafted ED+SKL (30.52 % Still) ≈ Haque's 3D RAM (30.1 % Walking). Nobody, however, reports METRIC (mm) anthropometry from unprojected depth with a measured drift model; that part is new.
3. **Frame-quality gating is de facto standard since 2012 but has never been ablated.** Barbosa 2012 selected the frame with best skeleton confidence, nearest, "not cropped by the sensors fields of view"; Munaro 2014 selected samples by face detection; Karianakis 2018 removed frames "heavily occluded from the image boundaries or too far from the sensor". None quantifies clipping prevalence (manuscript: 74 %) or the gate's effect (28 vs 9 %) or height drift per metre (89.5 mm/m). Claim first QUANTIFICATION, not invention.
4. **Cohort-size curves exist (Andersson & Araujo 2015 on Kinect anthropometrics, 10 random galleries per size with 95 % CIs; Friedman 2022 log-linear law; Grother & Phillips 2004 / Bolle 2005 theory; MultiGait Table 5 subset matching).** Per-subject identifiability is the Doddington zoo (1998) / biometric menagerie (Yager & Dunstone 2010). No depth-ID or gait paper applying the menagerie was located (search tooling limited). "Which people are enrolled matters more than how many" is not stated anywhere found; claim it as an empirical observation framed by the menagerie, cite Andersson for the curve.
5. **RGB's within-session edge of ~+45 pp is already in print on BIWI:** Hafner et al. CVIU 2022, Table 1, RGB 89.0 ± 3.9 vs depth 44.5 ± 1.0 rank-1 (within-instance, no clothing change) — a 44.5 pp gap. Depth > RGB under clothing change at fixed pipeline is Karianakis 2018 on TUM-GAID (48.0 vs 41.8 single-shot). RGB collapse under clothing change is PRCC (ResNet-50 74.80 → 19.43) and LTCC (58.82 → 20.08). Cite all as replications; the manuscript's contribution is the three-sensor, same-pipeline ladder with the segmenter control.
6. **No prior use of an RGB-derived segmenter vs the sensor mask as a control was found** (Crossref/arXiv; S2/dblp unavailable). Closest: LTCC's "ResNet-50 (Parsing)" input baseline (19.87 → 7.51), TVRID RGB winners' "background erasing" augmentation, Karianakis's Kinect body-index crop. Claim as new, hedged.
7. **Patruno et al. PR 2019 report 97.84 % rank-1 on BIWI from 10-fold cross-validation over 11,780 frame instances of the session-1 videos only**, explicitly excluding the re-recorded test sequences because colour descriptors would fail. This is a peer-reviewed exemplar of the frame-random inflation the manuscript diagnoses (C1) — cite neutrally (their aim was short-term re-ID and they said so).

## C. What C3/C4 can still claim / must not claim

CAN claim (with the stated hedges):
- First measurement of the FOV-clipping mechanism in depth identification: fraction of walking frames that clip the body (74 %), height-feature drift with standing distance (89.5 mm/m vs 105 mm between-subject SD), and the gate's effect on cross-session accuracy (28 vs 9 %). Prior work applied comparable gates without ablation (Barbosa 2012; Munaro 2014; Karianakis 2018).
- Metric anthropometry in millimetres from unprojected depth + sensor mask as a cross-session baseline that beats THE MANUSCRIPT'S OWN CNNs on the same protocol; state explicitly that published depth CNNs (Haque 30.1 %; Karianakis 25.4 %) and hand-crafted depth descriptors (Wu 24.47 %; Munaro 22.4 %) report higher single-shot cross-session numbers on the standard BIWI protocol, and explain why the manuscript's protocol yields lower numbers.
- A fixed 2.5-s observation budget with top-k reporting: no BIWI work reports accuracy against observation time; prior multi-shot pooled whole sequences (Karianakis, Haque, Delécluse) or used k gallery images (Wu). Closest analogue in the literature is VR (Nair 2023: 10 s vs 100 s) and LidarGait's frame-count ablation.
- Cohort curve K = 4→28 WITH draw spread AND per-subject identifiability 0–100 % on cross-session depth ID; cite Andersson 2015 for the within-session Kinect curve, Grother/Bolle/Friedman for the expected shape, Doddington/Yager–Dunstone for the per-subject vocabulary. "Which people are enrolled matters more than how many" may be claimed as an empirical observation in this domain; it is not new as a concept (menagerie).
- RGB with its own DeepLabV3 segmenter = RGB with the sensor mask as a CONTROL: no prior use found; claim as new, note LTCC's parsing-input baseline and TVRID's background-erasing augmentation as the nearest practice.
- Same-pipeline RGB vs depth ACROSS sessions on BIWI (RGB edge +45 pp within, ~0 across) as a replication of Hafner (within) and Karianakis/TUM-GAID (across, depth > RGB), extended to three sensor technologies and attributed to clothing via the segmenter control.

MUST NOT claim:
- That anthropometry "beats every CNN" without the qualifier "in our protocol".
- That depth-only cross-session identification on BIWI is newly demonstrated, or that 43–47 % is competitive (Karianakis 50.0 %, Haque 45.3 %, Munaro 42.9 % multi-shot in 2014–2018).
- That frame-quality gating or multi-frame aggregation is introduced here.
- That "RGB's edge is clothing" is new (Wu 2017; Karianakis 2018; PRCC; LTCC; Liu 2025 heatmaps).
- That gallery-size decline is new (Grother & Phillips 2004; Bolle 2005; Friedman 2022; Andersson 2015).
- That per-subject identifiability heterogeneity is new (Doddington 1998; Yager & Dunstone 2010).
- Any comparison of the manuscript's numbers with Hafner 44.5 %, Patruno 97.84 %, Liu 91.4 %, or Delécluse 60.7 % as if those were cross-session depth-only results.
- Height as "the" leaking cue: Barbosa 2012 already found height the most informative soft biometric; MultiGait normalises height away and still identifies across sessions, so height is neither necessary nor new.
